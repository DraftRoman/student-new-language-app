# Role Profile: Lead / Principal Product Owner
**Product area:** Language Learning Platform (iOS · Android · Web)
**Document type:** Internal role profile
**Status:** Active

---

## 1. Role Purpose

The Lead Product Owner owns the **end-to-end product vision, roadmap, and value delivery** for our cross-platform language learning product. The role is accountable for translating a long-term vision into a prioritized, evidence-based backlog that engineering, design, and data teams can execute with confidence — and for ensuring that what we ship is genuinely **engaging, lightweight, habit-forming, beginner-friendly, visually clean, and motivational** for learners.

At Lead / Principal level, this role is expected to set the **standard of product craft** for the team: how we discover, prioritize, decompose, experiment, and decide.

---

## 2. Product Context

We are building a mobile-first language learning platform comparable in category to Duolingo. The product helps users acquire foreign languages through:

- Gamified lessons
- Spaced repetition
- Quizzes
- Listening exercises
- Speaking exercises
- Personalized progression

**Target platforms:** iOS, Android, Web.

**Product feel — non-negotiables.** Every decision, from epic-level scope to micro-interactions, must defend these qualities:

| Quality | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Engaging | Pulls the learner back; rewarding short loops |
| Lightweight | Low cognitive load, fast launch, short sessions |
| Habit-forming | Streaks, reminders, spaced repetition rhythms |
| Beginner-friendly | Zero-prerequisite onboarding, gentle ramps |
| Visually clean | Minimal chrome, clear hierarchy, no clutter |
| Motivational | Progress feedback, milestones, positive tone |

---

## 3. Scope of Ownership

The Lead PO is the **single accountable owner** for:

1. **Product vision** for the language learning experience across all platforms.
2. **Roadmap** — what we build, in what sequence, and why.
3. **Product backlog** — the prioritized list of requirements for future product development.
4. **Requirements artifacts** — PRDs, epics, user stories, acceptance criteria.
5. **Discovery quality** — that decisions are grounded in CustDev, market data, and analytics, not opinion.
6. **Experimentation discipline** — that we test before we scale.

---

## 4. Core Responsibilities

### 4.1 Product Vision & Strategy

- Define and continuously refine the **product vision** for the language learning platform across iOS, Android, and Web.
- Translate vision into a **multi-horizon strategy** (now / next / later) that the whole team can articulate.
- Analyze **market trends** in EdTech, language learning, gamified learning, and adjacent categories.
- Maintain a working understanding of competitors (direct and indirect) and a point of view on where the category is moving.
- Own **pivot decisions**: detect signals that the current vision is misaligned with user reality or market reality, propose a revised direction, and lead the team through the transition.

### 4.2 Roadmap Ownership

- **Develop and maintain the product roadmap** for the platform.
- Prioritize features and enhancements against customer needs, business outcomes, and technical reality.
- Sequence work to balance habit-forming user value, platform health (iOS / Android / Web parity), and learning quality.
- Communicate the roadmap clearly to engineering, design, data, leadership, and any other stakeholders — and keep it honest as evidence changes.

### 4.3 Backlog & Prioritization

- Own the **product backlog** as the prioritized list of requirements for future development.
- Apply prioritization frameworks deliberately and fit-for-purpose:
  - **RICE** — for comparing initiatives at scale (Reach · Impact · Confidence · Effort).
  - **ICE** — for lightweight, fast scoring during discovery.
  - **MoSCoW** — for scope negotiation inside a release or epic (Must / Should / Could / Won't).
  - **Kano** — for understanding which features delight vs. satisfy vs. are basic expectations (critical for a gamified, motivational product).
- Make trade-offs visible and defensible. Every "yes" implies a "not now" — the backlog reflects both.

### 4.4 Discovery, Research & Analytics

- Run **Customer Development (CustDev)** cycles: structured user interviews, problem validation, solution validation.
- Conduct **market and competitor analysis** — both regular cadence reviews and ad-hoc deep dives when the landscape shifts.
- Drive **collaborative technical research with engineering before writing code** — feasibility spikes, architecture options, build-vs-buy reviews, risk surfacing — so that the team commits to scope with eyes open.
- Ensure **data integration** with the product workflow: that we instrument what we ship, that dashboards exist for every meaningful surface, and that decisions are observable.

### 4.5 Requirements & Decomposition

- Author **comprehensive Product Requirements Documents (PRDs)** for major initiatives, and **agile epics with user stories** for delivery.
- **Decompose hard, large epics into clear, actionable user stories**, applying:
  - **360-view thinking** — covering happy path, edge cases, error states, platform differences (iOS / Android / Web), accessibility, localization implications, analytics events, and migration of existing users.
  - **Big-picture thinking** — each story should be traceable back to a roadmap theme and a measurable outcome.
- Every story has **clear acceptance criteria** and is independently testable.
- Stories are written so a new engineer or designer joining the team can understand both *what* and *why*.

### 4.6 Cross-functional Collaboration

- **Engineering** — Pair early on technical research, feasibility, and trade-offs; treat engineers as discovery partners, not implementation resources.
- **Design** — **Design collab** is a first-class workflow: PO and design co-define problems, co-review research, and co-shape solutions. Visual cleanliness and beginner-friendliness are owned jointly.
- **Data / Analytics** — Co-design instrumentation; ensure every release ships with the metrics that prove or disprove its hypothesis.
- **Leadership / Stakeholders** — Translate strategy up, translate constraints down. Surface risks early.
- **Users** — Stay in direct contact with learners; CustDev is a continuous practice, not a quarterly project.

### 4.7 Delivery, Experimentation & Process Excellence

- Operate fluently in **Agile / Scrum / Kanban** — apply the framework that fits the team's current context, not the one written on a poster.
- Run a disciplined **experimentation and testing** practice:
  - Hypothesis-driven feature work.
  - A/B tests on high-traffic surfaces.
  - Pre-registered success criteria; honest reads, including null results.
- Apply **Six Sigma** thinking to reduce variance and defects in critical user journeys (onboarding, lesson completion, streak preservation, paywall flow). Use DMAIC where appropriate to investigate and fix systemic problems.

## 5. Required Skills & Competencies

### 5.1 Product Craft
- Strong **Product Vision** and **roadmap** authorship.
- Writing high-quality **PRDs**, epics, and **user stories** with acceptance criteria.
- Decomposing large, complex features using **360-view** and **big-picture** thinking.

### 5.2 Prioritization
- **RICE**, **ICE**, **MoSCoW**, **Kano** — used deliberately, not ritually.
- Ability to defend trade-offs to engineering, design, and leadership.

### 5.3 Research & Analytics
- **CustDev** — structured customer development interviews.
- Qualitative + quantitative feedback synthesis.
- Market and competitor analysis.
- **Data integration** into the product process.

### 5.4 Delivery Frameworks
- **Kanban** — practical fluency, framework-agnostic mindset.

### 5.5 Process Excellence
- **Six Sigma** — applied to user journey reliability and defect reduction.

### 5.6 Collaboration
- **Collaborative technical research with engineering before writing code**.
- **Design collab** as a co-creation practice.

### 5.7 Experimentation
- Hypothesis-driven product development.
- A/B testing and structured experiment design.

## 7. Success Indicators

The role is healthy when:

- The **vision** is articulated clearly enough that any team member can pitch it in two sentences.
- The **roadmap** is current, prioritized, and trusted across functions.
- The **backlog** has no zombie items — every story has a known reason to exist or is removed.
- **CustDev cadence** is continuous; user voice is present in every major decision.
- Every shipped initiative has a **hypothesis, a metric, and a read** — including the ones that failed.
- Engineering reports being involved **before** scope is locked, not after.
- Design reports co-ownership of problems, not just being handed solutions.
- The product continues to feel engaging, lightweight, habit-forming, beginner-friendly, visually clean, and motivational as it scales.


---

## 8. What This Role Is Not

To keep the role boundary clear:

- **Not a project manager.** Delivery coordination is supported, but the PO owns *what* and *why*, not Gantt charts.
- **Not a backlog secretary.** Prioritization is an act of strategy, not transcription.
- **Not a solo decision-maker.** Decisions are evidence-based and made with engineering, design, and data — but the PO is accountable for the call when alignment isn't possible.
- **Not the UX designer.** Design collab is a partnership; the PO does not own visual or interaction design.
- **Not the engineering manager.** Technical research is collaborative; architecture and implementation ownership stays with engineering.
